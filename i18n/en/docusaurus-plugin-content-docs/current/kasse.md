# Selling at the till

This page covers what happens at the till itself — what your team does on the night. For how a device becomes a till in the first place, see [Connecting devices](./geraete.md).

:::tip Try it first
While the event is in **test mode** you can run through the whole thing: up to 25 orders cost nothing. Activating the event deletes those test orders but keeps your setup. A banner at the top reminds you: "Test mode — orders are deleted on activation".
:::

## The screen at a glance

The till has three columns:

- **Left**, the categories of your range, for example *Drinks* and *Food*.
- **Middle**, the products in the chosen category, each with a price and a **+**.
- **Right**, the basket with subtotal, discount vouchers and the total.

The top bar shows the till's name, your organisation and the running event. On a phone the columns stack; the basket opens from the bar along the bottom, which carries the item count and total.

## Taking an order

1. **Pick a category** on the left.
2. **Add products** with the **+** on the card. Each further tap raises the quantity.
3. **Adjust quantities** in the basket with **−** and **+**. Taken to zero, the line disappears.
4. **Take payment** with **Cash** or **Card** at the bottom.

That completes the order. Anything bound for the kitchen or bar goes there immediately — see [Screens](./anzeigen.md) and [Printers](./drucker.md).

### Discount vouchers

**Add discount voucher** sits under the subtotal. The voucher is scanned or typed and comes off the total. Issuing vouchers is covered under [Discount vouchers](./rabatt-bons.md).

### Discarding a basket

**Empty basket**, below the payment button, throws away an order in progress. Nothing is saved and nothing is forwarded.

## Running tabs instead of paying at once

An event can be switched from immediate payment to **table service** (in the event settings, under *Ordering mode*). Then it works differently:

1. On start the till asks for a **table number** — `5` or `A1`, say.
2. Orders accumulate against that table instead of being paid one by one.
3. **Open tabs** lists every running table with its total.
4. At the end the table is settled there, **by cash or card**, and closed.

Part payments are possible; the overview then shows how much has already been paid.

## When the connection drops

The till keeps taking orders without a network and sends them on once it is back. What you should **not** overlook: a device permanently showing **Disconnected** is not reaching the kitchen or the screens live. Check the Wi-Fi and signal where that device stands.

## Signing a till out

**Sign out**, top right, releases the device from the organisation. It has to be [connected again](./geraete.md) afterwards — not something to do between customers. To merely lock the screen, lock the tablet itself.
